## The World on One Page

Humanity has learned to move between the stars almost instantly. A ship neither accelerates nor turns into data: it changes its spatial reference, traveling along an already existing path through the multiversal layer.

A path can be chosen only with the help of an anchor — a specially created pair of entangled particles. One half must be physically carried to the far end by ordinary flight: this takes decades or centuries. An anchor cannot be carried through another channel: a transition destroys the very entanglement on which the anchor rests. Civilization therefore expands in slow waves, while within already linked territory transitions can be nearly instantaneous.

Communication is just as unhurried. There is the atom-bit channel: a constant stream of hydrogen and helium atoms along a dedicated link anchor, but it requires specialized equipment. Where there is no equipment, data is sent as a packet, like a flash drive across hundreds of light-years.

In this world there are no plot conveniences lying around. A lost anchor means years of isolation. A failed second pulse means losing yourself. Every transition leaves a trace. Stories grow out of these constraints:

- A ship reached its destination but failed to lock in.
- An expedition deployed its anchors, everything works — but no one is left on Earth.
- A colony lost its only anchor and is waiting three hundred years for help.
- A hub decides which messages reach the neighboring colony.

The author of the concept deliberately writes no plots: he designs a place where other people's fates unfold. Your story can be written here.

## About the Document

The document describes a speculative world picture built on one unifying primary assumption and a set of related postulates, and then on the consequences of those. It is a foundation for writers: it lets them build plots without breaking the internal logic of the world. The concept is not presented as a description of the real Universe; its value here lies in its coherence, fruitfulness, and compatibility with known observations where such observations apply.

**The structure runs from physics to consequences to layered additions:**
- **Part I. Basic Principles** — the physics of the world.
- **Part II. Consequences** — logical conclusions from the principles. The single exception is Section 29: it describes a future discovery that the principles permit but do not entail.
- **Part III. Layers** — economics and other constructs built on top of the consequences. Optional: a particular civilization may organize itself differently.
- **Part IV. State of the Concept** — open questions, position, second-order hypotheses, summary.
- **Part V. Creative Principles and the Sandbox** — pitches, canon rules, the chronicle, and terms of use for authors.

**Versioning of the basis.** Parts I and II constitute the basis. It is fixed by versions: changes are made only through a new version recorded in the version history. A new version must not break already canonical works; if that is unavoidable, the version specifies how to bring them into compliance. Canonicity is always determined relative to a specific version of the basis.

**How to read.** A reader needs only "The World on One Page" and the pitches (Section 38). A writer needs Parts I, II, and V. A canon arbiter needs the whole document.

**Terms.**
- **Anchor** — a pair of entangled particles that defines a path. An anchor has two roles: a **transit anchor** (an external anchor buoy or mini-station in open space that receives arriving objects) and a **link anchor** (a separate pair inside a communications module).
- **Courier anchor** — a transit anchor on a mobile buoy next to an expedition's cargo; its partner stayed home (Section 25).
- **Communications module** — an atom-bit communications device with its own link anchor; on a ship it is undocked before a transition.
- **Stabilizer** — a device that protects an anchor particle from decoherence.
- **Modulators** — devices that act on the states of an object during a transition.
- **AI synchronizer (synchronizer)** — a system that aligns the states of an object with the path.
- **First and second pulses** — capturing the channel and locking in at the destination.
- **Atom-bit channel (microchannel)** — continuous two-way communication via a stream of atoms along a link anchor.
- **Packet probe** — an extremely simple craft carrying a data medium (a "flash drive").
- **Hub** — a network node through which intercolonial traffic passes.

## Part I. Basic Principles

### 1. The Multiversal Layer

The world consists of many physically existing universes connected by a common physical medium — the **multiversal layer**. This is not a mathematical abstraction and not a corridor located next to our space, but a separate level of reality not described by ordinary three-dimensional geometry. It has its own metric: two points that are distant in ordinary space may be close along a path through the layer.

Universes share a common origin (conventionally, a single primordial event), so their fundamental laws are largely similar, but their histories and states need not coincide. Interaction between an individual universe and the layer is normally weak. It is precisely this weak manifestation that makes background traces of interuniversal processes possible, without turning every universe into a freely permeable medium.

### 2. The Unified Temporal Field

A unified temporal field operates in all universes, with a common direction from past to future. Gravity and relative motion can alter the local rate of time, but a transition through the multiversal layer is not in itself time travel. Closed timelike loops are impossible in the canon.

This is a stand-alone postulate of the model, introduced to preserve causality for transitions that look faster-than-light from the standpoint of ordinary space. It is not derived from existing relativity theory; it is an internal rule of the world.

### 3. Spin, Path, Entanglement, and Fluctuations

In the model, every particle is connected to the multiversal layer. **Spin** is treated here as the carrier or marker of that connection: through its spin state, a particle correlates with the paths that join states across different universes. This is a special rule of the fictional model, not a claim that in real physics spin literally travels along hidden routes.

**Natural entanglement.** In the basic make-up of the world, natural entanglement arises predominantly between particles whose states correlate with different universes. Natural entanglement between particles of the same universe is extremely rare or practically nonexistent. This is one of the world's founding assumptions; it differs from modern quantum physics, where entanglement can also arise between systems within a single universe.

**Artificial entanglement.** Technology can create an entangled pair within a single universe. Such a pair does not lay a new path from scratch: it sets the conditions under which an existing path through the multiversal layer becomes addressable and suitable for transition. The corridor is not a separate tube or a new region of space, but managed access to a path in the layer.

**The infinite tree of paths.** Without an addressing condition, a state is connected to many possible paths between universes; the choice of a specific route is undefined. Entanglement narrows this tree to a specific pair of states and lets the system select a path. Entanglement itself carries no messages and does not replace a physical communication channel.

**Multiuniversal manifestation.** A particle's state may have correlated manifestations in several universes. Within the model, this does not necessarily mean that a full-fledged separate copy of the same particle resides in each universe: locally, only a certain aspect of its state may manifest — in particular, the spin state. If a given universe has no corresponding entangled particle, the passage of the spin through the layer may leave there a weak, short-lived trace.

**Quantum fluctuations.** In this concept they are interpreted as traces of the spin's passage through the multiversal layer. They are not stable local particles and do not form a controllable channel, but they can manifest as a weak background. This is an internal interpretation of the model, not an established explanation of quantum fluctuations in real physics.

### 4. The Anchor

An **anchor** is an entangled particle (or system of particles), one half of which is at the point of departure and the other at the point of destination. It performs three related functions:
- **Spatial coordinate** — where the object is to be locked in.
- **State reference** — to which universe, and to which of its states, this point is bound. A geometric coordinate alone is not enough: the path passes through a practically infinite number of universes.
- **Computational filter** — it reduces the infinite tree of paths to a single route (Section 3).

The third function underpins the first two: the coordinate and the reference are defined only because the path is defined. The reference is fixed when the pair is created.

The anchor is two-way: transition is possible in both directions (symmetry of entanglement). It is not consumed by transitions, so a single anchor serves many times. Each pair of anchors is a separate channel, and the channels are independent of one another. Hence the division of labor: **the anchor is infrastructure, the synchronizer is the operating system** (Section 9). The main limitation is not the anchor itself but the synchronizer's ability to use it properly.

**Two roles of an anchor.** A transit anchor stands in open space and receives arriving objects (Section 7). A link anchor is a separate pair inside a communications module; it serves only the stream of atoms (Section 21). Both are produced the same way (Section 5) and both are subject to Section 6.

### 5. Creating and Maintaining an Anchor

For an anchor, entanglement is created artificially through a controlled interaction of particles in a single laboratory. Within the model this is a technological exception to the rule that natural entanglement within one universe almost never arises. The pair is created in one place, after which one half is physically delivered to the target system by ordinary means — years or centuries. Therefore the first access to a remote system always requires an ordinary flight, and a faster-than-light route appears only after the anchor is installed. A possible future exception is the addressed entanglement of Section 29.

Each half of an anchor requires a **stabilizer**: a device that isolates the particle from external influences and maintains correlation with its partner. The main risk is decoherence: if entanglement is destroyed, the anchor stops working. Partial damage can be repaired by re-synchronizing the stabilizers; full decoherence is irreversible without bringing the particles together again — that is, without a new anchor.

An anchor is assembled from a stabilizer, an isolation system, a power system, and a synchronization system. In colonies without developed industry it can be installed, but not assembled.

### 6. An Anchor Cannot Be Carried Through a Channel

You can place two anchors in the same system, but you cannot send a new anchor through an existing channel. The first pulse locks the states of the carried object's particles relative to the particle on the far side. When the state of an entangled particle is locked to a new anchor, its connection to its partner is destroyed: the particle arrives intact, but ordinary.

**Why this happens:** The first pulse forcibly locks the states of the carried object's particles, fitting them to the metric of the path. For a macroscopic object this is controlled synchronization that preserves its structure. But for fragile quantum entanglement, such rigid fixation of state amounts to a measurement that instantly destroys the correlation (collapses the entanglement). As a result, the particle arrives at the destination physically intact, but loses its bond with its partner and becomes an ordinary particle.

An anchor cannot be carried through a channel: transition destroys the very entanglement that makes an anchor an anchor.

**Consequences:**
- **Delivering a new anchor** always means the ordinary route: an expedition of years or centuries. This is not a separate rule but a consequence of the transition mechanics.
- **No exponential rollout.** Otherwise civilization would blanket the galaxy in a couple of years: plant, jump, plant again. Expansion remains wavelike (Section 18). The only exception is within already linked territory, and only after the discovery of Section 29.
- **No "secret network of anchors."** A smuggled anchor was physically delivered by someone (Section 20).
- **Short jumps between already placed markers do work.** If a system has several anchors once delivered by ordinary means, moving between them is a small-scale task.
- **The communications module does not pass through a transition together with the ship.** It contains its own link anchor, which the first pulse would destroy as a measurement. So the module is undocked before the transition (Section 10).


### 7. An Anchor in Open Space

An object emerges from a transition at the anchor's point. If another object (a station, a planet, a ship) is nearby, the consequences are catastrophic. The atoms of the two objects end up in the same place, bonds tear, and both structures are destroyed. Even without that, the velocity difference produces a collision at cosmic speed (Section 14).

Anchors are always placed in open space, at a computed distance from any objects. Not as a rule, but because otherwise a transition would kill both the arrival and the thing it arrived at.

**Consequences:**
- **Two-tier transport.** The layer handles the great distances; conventional navigation handles the last few thousand kilometers. Both parts are needed.
- **The vulnerability window.** Between "we transitioned" and "we are at the dock," the ship travels on ordinary engines without transition protection. A ready-made scene for interception.
- **Passive protection of stations.** If the enemy has no anchor in your system, it cannot jump to your station.
- **A covert anchor near a station** is a weapon. But delivering such an anchor takes years or centuries, and the station's position relative to it remains unknown until the moment of transition.
- **Stationary and mobile anchors.** A stationary anchor is fixed relative to a planet or star. A mobile one is placed on a separate platform (an "anchor buoy") that moves with its own vector. A transit anchor is a self-contained piece of hardware (a buoy or mini-station), not attached to crewed modules. Neither a stationary nor a mobile anchor is mounted on the hull of a ship or station: the arrival would end up inside it.

### 8. Macroscopic and Biological Objects

Any object is a huge number of interrelated states (atoms, molecules, electronic states, bonds, macrostructure) that must be mutually consistent. A ship cannot be moved as a point: the whole of it must be synchronized. The more complex the object, the harder the synchronization.

A biological object is above all structure: molecular bonds, chemical gradients, neural networks, membrane potentials. It is defined by the mutual arrangement of atoms, not by the spins of individual particles. A transition locks the spins for passage through the channel, but does not sever bonds and does not rearrange atoms.

- **Nominal transition:** the heart does not skip a beat, neural connections do not tear, processes are not interrupted.
- **Emergency transition:** damage exactly proportional to the synchronization lost. A few atoms — imperceptible; a group of modulators failing — tissue damage; total collapse — death.

The state itself does not matter. A person in a coma, under anesthesia, or in cryosleep is carried the same way. Only the number and complexity of states matter.

### 9. The Synchronizer: AI and Modulators

A human being cannot govern that many states. The modulators act on the states of the object's constituents. The AI synchronizer reads and aligns the object's state in real time, computes the control signal, transmits it to the modulators, holds the object in an aligned state, monitors the transition, computes the reverse action, locks the object relative to the anchor, and breaks the original synchronization. Anchor stabilizers (Section 5) are different devices.

The AI is not a navigator or an interface but part of the movement mechanism, on a par with the anchor. It decides which channel to use, what degree of synchronization is attainable, where heightened attention is needed, how to allocate resources, and when to fire the second pulse (not by the clock, but by the object's state relative to the anchor). The pilot sets the destination; the crew entrusts the AI not with navigation but with the integrity of their own bodies.

Of the two pulses, the second is computationally simpler but more sensitive to precision: it must read the reference of the far anchor without error (Section 10).

### 10. The Four Stages of a Transition

**I. Reading and synchronization.** The ship approaches the anchor buoy at the point of departure, establishes a technical link with it, reads the current metric data of the multiversal layer, and computes the precise parameters of the first pulse.

**II. Communications isolation.** The onboard AI completes its calculations and orders the communications module undocked. The module contains its own link anchor (Section 21): the first pulse would fix its entangled particles as a measurement and destroy them (Section 6), and such particles are scarce. Therefore the module physically separates from the ship, is shielded from the pulse, and remains at the point of departure. Ordinary radio is unaffected, but the ship has no atom-bit channel during transit: this is a "blind transit." On the arrival side, the ship connects to the communications module of the receiving station. A probe has no such module; it carries only data (Section 20).

**III. Capture (the first pulse).** The modulators shift the object from its ordinary state into an aligned one and capture the channel. This is not acceleration but a restructuring of state. The modulators generate a macrostructural pulse that compresses the object's states to fit the computed path metric, and initiate the transition through the layer.

**Transit.** Between the first and second pulses, the spatial reference changes. The object travels under a different metric and does not cover distance, drag, or inertial load; from the outside it looks almost instantaneous. The object remains physically continuous: it is not destroyed and reassembled, and information is not transmitted separately from matter. It is the physical state of the object itself that moves.

**IV. Lock-in (the second pulse).** The second pulse completes synchronization with the path and locks the object into the spatial reference set by the anchor. It works like a quantum "latch": a reading device (on a ship, the synchronizer; in a probe, a compact modulator) reads the state reference of the far anchor at the point of arrival and instantly locks the object in. This stage does not require colossal computing power, but it is critically dependent on reading accuracy: an error here is the most dangerous failure (Section 11). The second pulse does not assign the object a new energy state: all states are preserved relative to the anchor's frame of reference.

The journey is over not when the object is "in the right place," but when it has acquired a stable membership in the anchor's universe.

### 11. Lock-In Failure

If the object reaches its destination but the second pulse is not fired in time, it remains bound to the layer and loses full lock-in.

- **Partial loss of state:** some elements fall out of synchronization — structural damage, loss of atoms or bonds.
- **Incomplete synchronization:** the object is locked neither in the original nor in the new configuration; in the limit, it loses unambiguous spatial membership.
- **Multiversal dispersal:** in the final collapse, parts of the object acquire different terminal states and may end up bound to different universes. This is not an explosion but the loss of a unified space-state membership.

**Grading of failures:** one modulator — local desynchronization; a group of modulators — cascading loss of synchronization; the AI — disintegration of the object; an error in reading the reference during the second pulse — the most dangerous failure (not because of the volume of computation but because of precision); total collapse — dispersal.

### 12. The Transit Trace

After a transition, a quantum noise — a trace of the disturbed interaction with the paths of the multiversal layer — remains at the point of departure. In the base case, a fluctuation is a weak trace of the spin's passage through the layer; the transit of a large object leaves a stronger, more structured trace, because the synchronization involves many states.

A covert transition is practically impossible. The trace makes it possible to establish the fact of transit and roughly estimate its scale; accuracy depends on the instruments and the background noise.

**Reconnaissance and forensics.** Traces can be used to investigate incidents, illegal channels, and military operations.

**The noise floor** of active anchors affects sensitive equipment and can betray channel activity.

A background fluctuation and a transit trace belong to the same class of phenomena in the model, but differ in intensity and degree of organization.

### 13. Energy

No energy is spent on covering distance.

The object does not cross ordinary space; it changes reference along an existing path. All the object's energy states are preserved relative to the frame of reference set by the anchor. A transition changes the object's spatial coordinate, but not its energy state: energy is neither lost, nor zeroed, nor converted into transition energy.

**Three things must be distinguished:**
- **Transition energy as the covering of ordinary distance** — practically absent: the object uses an existing path rather than traversing the whole span in ordinary space.
- **Synchronization energy** — needed for measurements, computation, running the AI, the modulators, cooling, and stabilization. The synchronizer is not a perpetual-motion machine.
- **The object's own energy state** — in a nominal transition it is preserved entirely relative to the anchor's frame of reference; only the spatial reference changes.

The cost of a transition is determined by the complexity of the object, the number of states to be aligned, the required precision, the power of the equipment, and the preparation time. Within the model, it depends primarily not on ordinary distance but on complexity and on the existence of a working anchor.

**The energy of interuniversal processes.** This is a separate level, not identical to either the object's energy or the energy of running the synchronizer. It has nothing to do with the energy of transitions; hypotheses about it are moved out of the basis into Section 35.

### 14. Momentum, Velocity, and Causality

**State of motion and frame of reference.** A transition changes where the object is, not its state of motion. All energy states, including those connected with motion, are preserved relative to the frame of reference set by the anchor. The anchor therefore serves not only as the destination coordinate but also as a stable point of reference for preserving the object's state.

**Geometric translation of states.** If the anchor's physical particle is taken as the fixed origin of a local Cartesian coordinate system with X, Y, Z axes, then in a transition the macro-object is projected into an absolutely identical spatial point and vector orientation relative to the far anchor's axes. Coordinates, linear velocity vector, angular momentum of rotation, internal temperature, and any other thermodynamic and kinetic parameters are preserved in the local matrix "one to one."

**No common frame of reference between stars.** Two star systems do not share one frame of reference: each has its own gravitational environment and its own orbital motions. For example, one anchor may be moving around its star at about 30 km/s, while another moves around its own at about 5 km/s. The ship preserves all its states of energy and motion relative to the anchor, but it emerges into the gravitational environment of the receiving system. Energy is neither conjured out of nothing nor lost: it stays with the ship, and its motion relative to the new star is determined by that star's gravity and the anchor's position.

**The dynamic difference between anchors.** The ship materializes with the parameters of the origin anchor. If the anchors have different self-motion vectors or axial rotations, the entire difference of inertial and centrifugal forces manifests at the moment of the second pulse. Navigation must quickly compensate for it with engines.

**The gravitational sling.** Since the ship emerges into a new gravitational environment with its states preserved, anchors can be used as directed cosmic slings: the ship accelerates relative to anchor A, transitions, and finds itself in the gravitational field of the receiving system with a given vector relative to anchor B. Navigation uses this as an ordinary gravitational maneuver, choosing the anchor and the moment of transition. The gain is determined by the motion and gravity of the receiving system, not by energy "from nothing."

**Compensating for vector differences.** If you need to stop at a station, a transition between anchors with very different vectors requires fuel to compensate for the difference — that is, it is expensive. The sling and compensation are two sides of the same property.

**Anchors in one system.** Within a single system, anchors share the star's frame of reference, and anchors on different orbits yield velocity differences relative to it. Whether to use this as a free orbital maneuver or to restrict it remains an open question (Section 34).

An exit cannot be made instantly unnoticeable: time is needed for maneuvering and docking. This is the window for interception.

A transition does not abolish sub-light navigation; it replaces it on the longest leg.

**Velocity and causality.** The transition is faster than light relative to ordinary space. But this is not motion faster than light through ordinary geometry: the object uses a different metric. Time still runs only forward (Section 2), so faster-than-light travel is possible without travel into the past.

### 15. A Transition Is Not a Weapon

The idea: jump to an anchor next to an enemy station at enormous speed and ram it. This is nearly impossible.

- The anchor gives no picture of the terrain. Only the point is known; where the station is at the moment of arrival is unknown, and data about the system go stale by years.
- The vector is set before the transition, in the system of departure, while the direction toward the target in the destination system is unknown.
- A blind aim is not an attack but a lottery with catastrophic odds against the attacker himself.

You can aim only with reconnaissance, and reconnaissance is possible in only two ways: a scout ship able to return (expensive, and it leaves a trace, Section 12), or your own people and sensors in the target system, transmitting data over a channel (Sections 20–21). Such an operation requires your own anchor in someone else's system and is noticeable. This is not impossibility — it is price.

### 16. Scaling: Zone, Throughput, Limit

The main constraint is not distance but the number of states that must be held in alignment. A small object needs a weak synchronizer; a large ship needs a powerful one. A complex structure is harder than a simple one of the same weight. Damage to the synchronizer raises the risk regardless of distance.

The mass carried in a single transition is limited by the synchronizer's power: a low-power one will carry tons, a powerful one thousands of tons. Scaling is an engineering problem. Whether there is an absolute limit is unknown (Section 34).

The **transition zone** is the region near the anchor within which an object can be captured or extracted; its size depends on the power of the equipment. **Transition throughput** is a series of consecutive transitions (a cargo stream); it requires continuous power supply to the synchronizer.

### 17. The Central Principle

The anchor sets the spatial coordinate and the frame of reference. The modulators provide the synchronization of the object with the path. The AI aligns the object's constituents. The first pulse captures the channel. The path carries out the transition. The second pulse completes the synchronization and locks the object into the new spatial reference. All the object's energy states are preserved relative to the anchor's frame of reference. Energy is spent on controlling the transition, not on covering ordinary distance.

It is not energy that moves the object — it is the aligned state that makes it possible to change the spatial reference.

Distance determines the need for an anchor. The complexity of the object determines the computational cost of the transition. The state of the object determines the possibility of a safe lock-in.

## Part II. Consequences

These are logical conclusions from the basic principles. The single exception is Section 29: it describes a future discovery that the principles permit but do not entail.

### 18. Wavelike Expansion

Civilization expands in waves.

**The first wave** — ships travel the ordinary way, hundreds of years, carrying the halves of future anchors (Section 6). On arrival, the anchor goes live, and a channel appears between the new colony and the metropolis. Thus the infrastructure map arises: first the nearest system, then the next.

**Subsequent waves** use the channels differently. Ships without anchor cargo travel along existing channels to the edge of the network. If industry exists there (Section 5), pairs are entangled at the frontier, and the ordinary flight begins from there rather than from the metropolis. The anchor cargo itself cannot pass through a channel (Section 6), so each new system beyond the frontier remains a century of ordinary travel away.

Within linked territory, movement is instantaneous. The network's frontier crawls at the speed of ships.

An anchor is an investment: installing it requires an ordinary flight, but afterward the route exists permanently.

### 19. Loss of an Anchor

The loss of an anchor is a catastrophe for a colony. The anchor is its only link to the metropolis: without it there is no transition, no communication, no supplies. Recovery requires a new ship traveling the ordinary way — hundreds of years.

**Causes of loss:**
- decoherence due to stabilizer failure or aging (Sections 5, 26);
- physical destruction of the anchor (accident, war, sabotage);
- loss of the partner particle on the other side.

Destroying an anchor does not necessarily destroy the colony. It returns the colony to real cosmic isolation. If the colony is not self-sufficient, this can mean collapse.


### 20. Gray Channels, Physical Mail, and Probes

**Illegal channels.** An unregistered anchor creates a route the authorities do not know about. But delivering its half takes the same hundreds of years of ordinary flight (Section 6): "planted unnoticed" does not happen. Using a channel leaves traces: the transit trace (Section 12), fluctuations, interference, instrument errors. Anchor smuggling is therefore a separate industry.

**Physical mail.** Entanglement does not carry information, but the path itself can be used to move physical media. Without an anchor, this is a ship with records, letters, and data flying an ordinary course: slow, but without infrastructure. For cut-off colonies this is sometimes the only connection.

**The packet probe (a "flash drive").** If the recipient has no communications module and no link anchor (Section 21), data can be delivered as a packet. An extremely simple craft is sent through a transit anchor: a data medium and a compact modulator for the second pulse, which reads the anchor's reference. The computation and the first pulse are performed by external installations on the sending side, so the probe is cheap: the lock-in stage does not require onboard AI capacity (Section 10). This is a flash drive thrown across hundreds of light-years: one packet, no permanent connection.

- The probe is one-way: it cannot return by itself. The answer goes by a return probe, so both ends need stations capable of performing the computation and the first pulse.
- The probe has no communications module with entangled elements, so there is nothing for it to undock.
- The probe is small, has few states (Section 16), quick preparation, and a cheap transition.
- A weak trace (Section 12): mass mailings of probes are harder to spot than ships.
- Cheap but unreliable: a simple modulator may fail to lock in; data are lost or corrupted. Hence duplicated sendings, mail insurance, and cheap counterfeit probes (Section 27).
- The probe is physical: it can be intercepted on the way from the anchor to the station (Section 7).
- Compact modulators are made from the same scarce components as large synchronizers (Section 31), so scarcity hits the mail too.

The probe suits those who have no communications equipment, and one-off packets. A continuous stream is provided by the atom-bit channel (Section 21), but it requires specialized equipment.

### 21. The Atom-Bit Channel (Microchannel)

The transition mechanics works not only for ships but also for single atoms: the smaller the object, the simpler the synchronization (Section 16). The payload here is not a ship but an atom. This is the same anchor mechanics, not new physics.

**What is needed.** The channel requires specialized equipment: a communications module with its own link anchor (a separate entangled pair), mass-spectrometric detectors, and an AI for decoding. The link anchor is not the same as the transit anchor: it sits inside the communications device and serves only the stream of atoms, while the transit anchor stands in open space and receives arriving objects (Sections 4, 7). Without such equipment, only the packet probe remains (Section 20).

**Principle.** The transmitter continuously sends atoms through the path of the link anchor. The receiver illuminates the arrival zone with a pulse that reproduces the anchor's reference: this is not a measurement but support of the zone, so that atoms arrive where they should. An atom arrives — the mass spectrometer determines its type — a bit is read.

**Hydrogen and helium.** Hydrogen (mass 1) is a one; helium (mass 4) is a zero. These are the two simplest and most common atoms, distinguishable by mass by a factor of four. Mass spectrometry is a classical measurement requiring no quantum coherence.

**Two-way mode.** Each side simultaneously sends its own stream and receives the incoming one: the symmetry of entanglement lets the streams pass toward each other without mutual interference. The last leg on each side uses ordinary communication: from the detector to the station, data travel at the speed of light.

**The communications device.** It is a small autonomous unit, not a station: no large-object arrivals should happen near the point where atoms arrive (Section 7).

**Bandwidth.** With precision pulsed illumination of the arrival zone, the bottlenecks become the speed of the mass-spectrometric detectors and the computing power of the decoding AI. This allows speeds comparable to broadband internet. But the channel links only a pair of devices, and link anchors are few (Section 22): the scarcity is not bandwidth but the number of lines.

**What it gives.** A continuous data channel instead of separate packets. A message travels under a different metric, faster than light relative to ordinary space, but without travel into the past (Section 2).

**What does not change.** Entanglement still does not transmit information: communication proceeds by moving atoms, not by measuring an entangled state. The channel works only between two link anchors. This is physical mail at the atomic level, not telepathy.

**What gives the trace away.** Every atom leaves a trace (Section 12), and its intensity depends on the number of particles. A high-density stream creates a powerful structured trace on the transmitting side, and the fact of transmission, together with its approximate volume, is easy to detect. Moreover, a sensitive sensor at the transmitter can in principle distinguish hydrogen from helium — that is, read the bits. How accurately remains an open question (Section 34). Eavesdropping is possible only by physically being nearby, but the fact of transmission is always given away by the trace.

### 22. The Telegraph Model

"Telegraph" here describes the structure of the network, not its speed. Each line is point-to-point between two entangled link anchors. You cannot send a message "to everyone," and you cannot create a line without physically delivering a particle (before the discovery of Section 29). A message does not pass through ordinary space, so it cannot be intercepted in transit. But the receiver can be seized, like a telegraph station, or the trace can be listened to near the transmitter (Section 21).

This is not the internet and not the telephone: there is no common network, only separate lines, and each takes years or centuries to build. Within a line, communication can be fast, but there are few lines.

Each line is a separate irreplaceable resource: lose the anchor and the connection is severed; it can be restored only by a new delivery. Communication lines are therefore a strategic asset: they are defended, fought over, and their loss is a catastrophe. The network grows slowly, line by line, like the telegraph network of the nineteenth century. A line's bandwidth depends on the equipment at its ends; the number of lines is limited by the scarcity of link anchors.

### 23. The Hub and Network Topology

A star topology is the natural consequence of how anchors are made. Pairs are entangled in the metropolis, where production and long-range ships exist, and each colony receives its half. The metropolis is the only point with channels to every colony. A direct channel between colonies A and B is possible only if a pair was entangled and one half delivered by ship — that is, over centuries and by a decision someone has to make (Section 24).

A message from A to B travels: A → metropolis → B. Two channels, and the metropolis sees everything.

**Dependence on the hub.** If the hub falls, all colonies are cut off from one another. The hub is the most heavily defended point of civilization.

**Power over information.** Whoever controls the hub can withhold a message, delay it, read it, and forward it altered.

**Physical monopoly.** The metropolis does not pass a law banning direct channels. It simply does not send particles and ships to create them, and a colony, as a rule, has neither long-range ships nor stabilizer production.

**The motive for decentralization.** The more colonies, the greater the load on the hub, the delays, and the cost of transit.

### 24. Decentralization and Colonial Independence

Colonies do not need to fight the hub for their routes: they can create their own anchors and send them to other systems. But they run into the same constraint — delivery goes the ordinary way, hundreds of years.

1. Colony A launches a ship carrying an entangled particle to colony B.
2. Two hundred years later the ship arrives, and a direct channel appears between A and B, bypassing the hub.
3. The metropolis loses control over this route.

The hub's monopoly is not political but physical. It rests on the fact that direct delivery of an anchor between colonies takes centuries.

Independence is possible, but slow. While the anchor is in flight, the hub knows of the plans and can interfere — for instance, by "losing" the launch clearance. The monopoly is temporary: the network slowly turns from a star into a mesh. The hub loses its monopoly but remains the largest node.

### 25. Expeditions

An expedition is a ship carrying hundreds or thousands of entangled particles — future anchors. It is not merely transport but an incubator of infrastructure.

**Cargo and crew.** A transition destroys entanglement (Section 6), so cargo with entangled particles cannot be carried through a channel: it travels the ordinary way, hundreds of years. But the expedition has a courier anchor — a pair, one half of which stayed home, while the other is installed on a mobile buoy next to the cargo (not on the ship's hull, Section 7). The ship can leave the cargo with the buoy, jump home, change the crew, make repairs, restock, jump back, and continue. The vector is preserved in a transition; velocity differences are damped by engines (Section 14).

The cargo can last three hundred years. People cannot.

**Two classes of cargo:**
- **Transit cargo** — can be passed through a channel: people, equipment, fuel, data. It does not need entanglement, so losing it does not matter.
- **Anchor cargo** — cannot go through a channel: entangled particles, stock for communication lines. Ordinary flight only.

**Risks to the cargo.** While the ship is home, the cargo hangs in the void alone: without crew, protection, or oversight. A stabilizer fails — the particle decoheres, and no one knows until the ship returns. A meteorite punctures the container — the anchors are lost. A flare from a nearby star destroys part of the entanglements. The more often the ship leaves for maintenance, the longer the cargo is unattended; the less often, the longer the crew goes without relief and repair.

**The emergency protocol.** If the courier anchor is lost, the ship picks from the cargo one of the anchors; which one is determined by the protocol's algorithm. The ship installs it on a mobile buoy as the new courier anchor: this anchor's partner remained in the metropolis, and the journey home is again possible. Word of what happened goes over the atom-bit channel if the communications module is intact, or by packet probe (Sections 20–21). Earth answers the same way.

Every anchor in the cargo is not only a future channel for a colony but also a spare emergency channel home.

**The price of the protocol.** Every anchor used as an emergency one is denied to a colony: out of a thousand, 999 remain, and one system turns out without a communication link to the hub. Three accidents in three hundred years — three fewer channels. Which anchor to sacrifice is a strategic decision: for the most promising system, or for the one that will get a channel through a neighbor?

Two non-renewable resources: the anchors for the colony and the courier anchor for the return. The loss of either is a catastrophe of a different scale, and the emergency protocol partially compensates for the loss of the courier anchor at the expense of the cargo ones.

### 26. Aging

Anchor stabilizers are not eternal. Over time the isolation degrades, precision falls, entanglement weakens. This is the main cause of the gradual loss of anchors in stable conditions. Periodic re-synchronization extends service life but not indefinitely: sooner or later the anchor must be replaced, and replacement requires a new pair (Sections 5, 6).

Synchronizers age too: the computing system degrades, modulators wear out, the number of errors and the probability of incomplete lock-in grow. A colony may have a fully functional anchor yet gradually lose the ability to pass large objects through it. Maintenance is therefore a matter of physical safety.

### 27. The Counterfeit Synchronizer

Cheap or counterfeit components operate unstably. This creates a new class of accidents: a device capable of performing a transition but incapable of guaranteeing lock-in.

A counterfeit synchronizer is the analogue of a faulty engine, only the consequences of failure are far graver.

Cheap counterfeit probes are lost more often (Section 20). For a crew, a counterfeit synchronizer means the risk of dispersal (Section 11).

### 28. A Philosophical Consequence

If an object, during a transition, is in an intermediate state between universes, belonging to a universe becomes not only a spatial but also a state property. The anchor is not just a coordinate but physical evidence of the object's belonging to a particular universe.

Therefore "home" is not a place but a set of anchors. If all the anchors are lost, home is lost, even if the same stars surround you.

**The religious question.** Does the object retain identity in the traditional sense? Is it the "same" person after the change of reference? Does a person have the right to "embed" themselves into a path of the layer? Some traditions will see an extension of the divine design; others, an interference with the nature of reality; still others, a technology that does not touch the spiritual sphere, since the object remains physically continuous.

**The ethical question.** Who is responsible if the synchronization is imperfect and some atoms do not return to phase? If a counterfeit synchronizer dispersed the crew, is that murder or an accident? Is civilization obligated to send an ordinary expedition to a colony cut off by the loss of an anchor, if the journey will take decades?

A technology that makes reality switchable poses the question of what it means to "be here" — and who answers for it.

### 29. Addressed Entanglement — the Apex

This is not a consequence of the principles but a second-order assumption: a possible future discovery. Its mechanism remains open (Section 34).

All the stories of this universe are stories before the equation: the age of the telegraph, the age of the hub, the age of long flights and lost expeditions. An age when every anchor is a treasure, every channel a struggle for independence, every loss a tragedy.

And somewhere in a laboratory — perhaps in a small colony on the edge of the network — someone finds a way to narrow the tree of paths in a targeted way, without prior physical delivery. Not new physics, but new mathematics of old physics. Just as Maxwell did not build the electromagnetic wave but wrote the equations that showed it exists.

**What the equation gives.** If at least one working anchor already exists between two systems, new entangled pairs can be created in a targeted way, through the existing channel, without physical delivery. One channel begets dozens.

The hub loses its monopoly, but slowly: only between systems that already have addressed-entanglement hardware.

**The second wave of expansion** is the network wave. Within linked territory the number of channels grows exponentially, because each new one is created through an existing one. The network turns from a star into a mesh in years, not centuries.

**What the equation does not give:**
- **The settling of new systems.** It still requires centuries of ordinary flight: on the other side there is as yet nothing, while hardware and particles are needed on both sides.
- **Direct transmission of information.** Entanglement still does not carry it; the only means of communication remain packet probes and the atom-bit channel.
- **A cheap anchor at the frontier.** There, an anchor still costs a century of flight.

Within the network — a dense, instantaneous, multipolar civilization. At the frontier — the same three hundred years of flight, the same lost expeditions, the same insurance pools. And the only way across is to fly the hundreds of years the ordinary way.

The equation violates not a single basic principle: the layer, the anchor, and synchronization remain.

## Part III. Layers

This is what civilization builds on top of the consequences: economics, politics, logistics. Each layer follows from the consequences but is not mandatory: a particular civilization may organize itself differently.

### 30. Economics

The economy is defined by two facts: a transition costs computation and synchronization energy (Section 13), and an anchor costs a century of delivery.

**The anchor is capital.** An anchor between two systems is not a device but infrastructure, like a bridge or a pipeline. The owner charges for transitions: for the complexity and mass of the object, for energy, for urgency.

**The AI is an operating resource.** Control over the production of synchronizers means control over the civilization's transport system. The synchronizer needs processors, quantum coprocessors, cooling, rare materials, repairs, and updates.

### 31. Supply Chains

Supply is two-tiered. The intersystem tier goes through anchors: goods are loaded into a container, pass through an anchor, and are unloaded in the target system. Fast, but it costs a transition plus the operator's margin. The intrasystem tier is ordinary transport. Where there is no anchor, only ordinary flight remains.

Not everything is worth carrying: cost grows with mass (Section 13), so bulk non-urgent freight such as ore is better produced locally. Data, medicines, and rare components go through transitions.

**The vulnerability of supply chains.** Synchronizers require raw materials from many systems: computing crystals from some deposits, cryogenic systems from others, rare-earth elements for modulators from still others, quantum coprocessors from fabs that depend on a few systems. One break in the chain, and synchronizer output falls.

War or disaster in a single system that mines a needed mineral can paralyze transit across an entire sector.

There is no need to destroy anchors or block routes: it is enough to disrupt the supply of one critical component, and within months the civilization begins losing the ability to pass large objects. Control over sources of raw materials matters more than control over routes, and sanctions against component makers work better than blockades.

### 32. The Economy of Complexity

Civilization does not pay for kilometers. It pays for the management of states.

In an ordinary civilization: distance → fuel → cost. Here: object complexity → computation → energy consumption → cost. Therefore:
- a small cargo can be cheap regardless of distance (probes, Section 20);
- a huge ship can be expensive even on a short route;
- upgrading the AI increases transport capacity;
- a shortage of computing components becomes a shortage of transport.

Energy remains a commodity: synchronization, production, and life support consume it. Whoever controls energy sources (star stations, for example) controls the economy, and the anchor operators are its clients.

### 33. The Economics of Communications

The atom-bit channel (Section 21) requires a dedicated link anchor and specialized equipment, and creates a separate market. Those without the equipment pay for packet probes (Section 20).

**The hub as a transit center** (Section 23): it charges for routing, like the Suez Canal or a rail junction.

**Channels as assets.** An anchor yields income: fees per bit, per message, per connection. Channel insurance, spare pairs, and capacity trading appear.

**Communication smuggling.** Illegal anchors create channels bypassing the hub: a black market for transit, underground repeaters.

**Information as a resource.** A line's bandwidth is set by its equipment, and the number of lines is limited by the scarcity of link anchors; a colony receiving more data gains scientific, economic, and military advantage. The hub controls this flow.

**Monopoly and competition.** While the hub is the only one, transit is expensive. When direct channels appear (Sections 24, 29), the price falls and the hub loses its margin.

## Part IV. State of the Concept

### 34. Open Questions

A list of questions the document does not yet answer. These are not necessarily contradictions: some are technical uncertainties of the world, others are places where a qualitative consequence does not yet have a quantitative description.

**Decisions made by rule of the world (version 8.3).** These are not conclusions from first principles but choices of the concept's authors:
- *Why a transition destroys entanglement.* The first pulse forcibly locks states and is equivalent to a measurement (Section 6).
- *The energy balance between anchors.* All states are preserved relative to the anchor; the ship's motion in the new system is determined by its gravity (Section 14).
- *Communication.* Two modes: the atom-bit channel with a dedicated link anchor, and the packet probe (Sections 20–21).

**Open questions:**
- **An absolute mass limit.** Is there a physical ceiling on the mass carried in one transition, or is it purely an engineering limit that recedes as synchronizers improve? (Section 16)
- **The mechanism of addressed entanglement.** How exactly does the narrowing of the tree of paths without physical delivery work? Section 29 postulates that it is possible but does not describe the mechanism. How does it differ from the known real-physics phenomenon of entanglement swapping? (Section 29)
- **The accuracy of eavesdropping on the atom-bit channel.** How accurately can a sensitive sensor at the transmitter distinguish hydrogen from helium in the trace? In theory yes; in practice it depends on the hardware and the distance. (Section 21)
- **Stabilizer degradation.** What is the real service life of a stabilizer in different conditions — from a protected hub to a drifting expedition buoy? Does the intensity of channel use affect the rate of degradation?
- **The speed of ordinary ships.** How fast are ordinary-flight ships? Two hundred, three hundred, five hundred years to the nearest system — the pace of expansion and the scale of the tragedies depend on it.
- **Counterfeit: the boundary of definition.** What distinguishes a "budget" synchronizer from a "counterfeit" one? Is there certification, and who performs it in a decentralized network?
- **Multiversal dispersal: observability.** Can dispersal be distinguished from destruction? If part of an object went to another universe, does a trace remain, or does it look like an ordinary loss?
- **The economics of an anchor: pricing.** How is the fee for a transition formed? A regulated tariff, a market price, or the monopolistic rent of the anchor's owner?
- **Transition insurance.** How does insurance against dispersal work? Who pays if a crew is lost because of a counterfeit synchronizer?
- **Addressed entanglement: side effects.** What else does the equation give besides creating new pairs? Does the structure of traces change? Does interception become easier?
- **Recovery after the loss of a hub.** If the hub is destroyed, how long does it take to rebuild the star topology from surviving direct channels? Is it possible without the metropolis?
- **The limit of decentralization.** How mesh-like can the network become before the latency of multi-hop routing outweighs the benefits of independence?
- **Anchors in one system.** Do anchors on different orbits provide a free orbital maneuver, or must it be restricted? (Section 14)
- **The communications module after undocking.** Where does the module remain, how does the ship restore atom-bit communication after arrival, and what happens if there is no receiving station? (Sections 10, 21)
- **The cosmological constant (hypothesis).** How can the distribution of the energy of spin processes across the layer be quantitatively tied to a small effective energy density within a single universe? (Section 35)
- **The layer's gravitational effect (hypothesis).** By what process can the layer affect gravity, why do such effects add up rather than cancel out, and how does this agree with gravitational lensing, the cosmic microwave background, and large-scale structure? (Section 35)


### 35. The Position "Under Physics"

The document builds a world from a primary assumption to consequences, not from plot to rules. This is a speculative method of constructing a possible world picture, not a claim of proven new physics.

**The primary assumption sets the make-up.** The multiversal layer and the paths existing in it are a fundamental part of the model. Spin, entanglement, and local traces describe ways of interacting with this structure.

**Consequences must not quietly become new independent assumptions.** If a phenomenon is claimed as a consequence, the text must show through which chain of basic principles it is derived. If an additional postulate is needed, it is stated openly.

**The layers are arbitrary.** Economics, logistics, and politics are built on top of the consequences, but a particular civilization may arrange itself differently. Authors are free in these layers as long as they do not break the rules of the world.

**Canon is coherence with the physics.** A work is canonical if it violates none of the basic principles and does not contradict already established canonical events. Conformity to the physics is mandatory. Conformity to a particular political or economic model is not.

**What the document does not do.** It does not describe the history of humanity, does not set a political map, does not define who wars with whom. It sets a place where this may happen, and the constraints within which it must happen.

**Second-order hypotheses (not part of the basis).** Two ideas are moved out of Parts I–II into this section: they do not follow from the principles automatically, and each needs a separate postulate, named openly. Authors may use them as background, but not as an established fact of the world, until they are moved into the hard canon by the procedure of Section 39.

- **The cosmological constant.** Postulate: the energy of interuniversal processes need not manifest entirely within one universe. If spin processes pass through the common layer, their energy may be distributed across the multiversal structure, leaving only a small effective share in any single universe. This is a possible origin of the small observed energy density. No quantitative derivation has been obtained: the document does not claim that a numerical value, an equation of state, or cosmological dynamics has been found.
- **The layer's gravitational effect ("dark matter").** Postulates: the layer's processes weakly affect the gravity of our universe; individual effects are negligible but add up; the intensity of the effect may depend on differences in matter distribution and spacetime structure between universes (black holes are treated only as a preliminary line of inquiry, not a proven factor). Then part of the effects attributed to dark matter would have a different source. The hypothesis does not claim that dark matter does not exist, and does not involve the transfer of matter between universes. To claim an explanation, it must reproduce the totality of data: galaxy rotation curves, gravitational lensing, cluster dynamics, large-scale structure, and the cosmic microwave background. A separate requirement is to explain lensing — the deflection of light, not merely the motion of matter. The mechanism is not yet defined.

A common assumed source does not mean that both problems are solved by one mechanism: the smallness of the cosmological constant and the effects attributed to dark matter are different physical problems.

### 36. Summary

"Quantum Paths" is built around one unifying assumption: reality includes a multiversal layer with already existing paths along which spin states link individual universes. Entanglement makes it possible to select a path in a targeted way; artificially created entanglement within a single universe opens the possibility of forming a transition corridor. From this foundation are derived anchors, wavelike expansion, two modes of communication (the atom-bit channel and the packet probe), hubs, decentralization, expeditions, the aging of equipment, and the social consequences that follow.

Second-order hypotheses (the cosmological constant, the layer's gravitational effect) are moved out of the basis and described in Section 35 as qualitative directions, not finished conclusions.

The concept remains speculative. Its possible scientific value depends on future formalization and testing; its artistic value does not depend on whether the original assumption turns out to be real. Sixteen open questions mark the technical and quantitative limits of the current version. Section 29 still describes a separate future discovery that changes the world's network architecture.

What follows: creative principles, story pitches, the canon-coherence model, and the terms of use.

## Part V. Creative Principles and the Sandbox

### 37. The Principle "Space Will Not Save You"

The document's main task is to make space a source of constraints, not a backdrop. In most fictional universes, space is scenery: beautiful, but with no bearing on the plot. Here every constraint is a potential plot.

**Constraints as engines:**
- An anchor cannot be carried through a channel — hence expansion is slow.
- Every transition leaves a trace — hence there are no covert operations.
- A failed second pulse means losing yourself — hence transition is a risk.
- An anchor in open space — hence between transition and docking there is a vulnerability window.
- A stabilizer ages — hence even a peaceful colony can lose its link.
- A counterfeit synchronizer — hence the economics of poverty kills people.

**What must not appear in the canon:**
- Faster-than-light communication without a link anchor.
- Travel into the past (Section 2).
- A transition without a trace.
- An anchor (transit or link) delivered through a channel, or a communications module that passed through a transition together with the ship.
- Movement that violates the preservation of states relative to the anchor (Section 14).
- Restoring a lost anchor without an ordinary flight (except under the conditions of Section 29).
- Transition as a weapon without reconnaissance and price.

If a plot requires breaking one of these rules, it is not canonical. This is not a ban on writing — it is the boundary of the canon.

### 38. Pitches

Fourteen first-iteration plots and one finale. Pitches are not finished works but points from which short stories, novellas, and novels may grow. The author of the concept does not write them; he offers them to others. The rules of the world on which a pitch rests are given in parentheses.

**First-iteration plots (before the equation):**

1. **The Last Anchor.** A colony loses its only anchor. The stabilizer has degraded, and there is no replacement. Until the expedition arrives — three hundred years. A story about people who know no help is coming and must decide: build an autonomous civilization or slowly fade. (Sections 19, 26.)

2. **Counterfeit.** A poor colony buys a cheap synchronizer from the gray market. The first transition goes fine. The second does not. The crew is dispersed. An investigation: who sold it, who knew, who is guilty — murder or negligence. (Sections 11, 27.)

3. **The Courier.** An expedition loses its courier anchor. The emergency protocol triggers: one of the cargo anchors becomes the channel home. But that means one system out of a thousand will not get its channel. Who decides which one, and how do you tell the system that was supposed to receive it? (Section 25.)

4. **The Trace.** A channel between two systems behaves strangely: traces stronger than usual, but no ships. Reconnaissance discovers that someone is sending mass probes — but why, and to whom? (Sections 12, 20.)

5. **Sabotage at the Frontier.** An expedition at the edge of the network. Someone is deliberately damaging the stabilizers of the cargo anchors. But who: a competitor, a political opponent — or is the automation simply glitching, and there is no sabotage? (Sections 25, 26.)

6. **The Hub.** The hub is the only node through which all messages pass. The hub's operator begins delaying, filtering, and rewriting messages. The colonies notice months later. How do you fight a monopoly on information when there is no direct channel and there will not be one for two hundred years? (Sections 23, 24.)

7. **Aging.** The stabilizer of an anchor at a distant station is degrading. The technician knows that in a year the anchor will die. There are no spare parts, and there will be no new pair. Re-synchronization is possible, but the stabilizer will survive exactly one attempt: a mistake destroys the entanglement for good. He must decide when and how to do it. (Sections 5, 26.)

8. **Interception.** A probe carries data through a channel. It can be intercepted on the way from the anchor to the station, if you know where the anchor is and get there in time. Someone knows. Where they learned it is another question. (Sections 7, 20.)

9. **Two Classes.** An expedition carries both transit and anchor cargo. An accident: part of the anchor cargo is lost. But the transit cargo is intact. The commander must decide: go back for new anchors (another hundred years), or fly on and plant what is left — one colony short. (Section 25.)

10. **Physical Mail.** A ship with records, letters, and data flies an ordinary course to a cut-off colony. Three hundred years. By the time it arrives, the colony may no longer exist. Or it may exist but no longer need the letters. Or it may need one particular letter — from a person three hundred years dead. (Section 20.)

11. **The Vulnerability Window.** A ship exits a transition at the anchor's point. Four hours to the station on ordinary engines. In those four hours anyone can intercept it: pirates, the military, competitors. Transition protection is gone. What does the crew do while the window lasts? (Sections 7, 14.)

12. **They Age Faster.** Stabilizers at several distant anchors are degrading noticeably faster than the model predicts. A physicist searches for the cause: a manufacturing defect, operating conditions, or something no one accounted for. The answer is left to the author, but it must not break the basic principles; if the author needs new physics, that is a question for the arbiter. (Sections 5, 26, 39.)

13. **No One Comes.** An expedition deployed its anchors, the channel works, everything is ready. But at the other end, back home, no one remains: civilization died while the ship was in flight. The anchor connects to an empty place. What does the crew do — the one holding priceless infrastructure with no one to use it? (Sections 6, 18, 25.)

14. **The Cargo That Aged Out.** The ship launched three hundred years ago, and its anchor cargo was designed for the technology of that time. Since then, standards for stabilizers and synchronizers have changed at home. The colony receives priceless anchors but must choose: adapt them to the new network at the cost of some pairs, or live in its own incompatible network. (Sections 5, 18, 26.)

**The finale of the first story arc:**

**The Equation.** A small colony at the edge of the network. A laboratory that, in the hub's opinion, does nothing important. A physicist working on a problem everyone considers theoretical: can the tree of paths be narrowed without physically delivering a particle? And one day — it works. Not new physics, but new mathematics of old physics. One channel begets dozens. The world changes. But not for everyone: at the frontier there are still the same three hundred years of flight. (Section 29.)

### 39. The Canon-Coherence Model

Canon is not primacy but coherence. A work becomes canonical not because it was published first or is better than others, but because it violates none of the basic principles and does not contradict already established canonical events.

**Two statuses of works:**
- **"Written in this world."** The author declares themselves that the work uses the world and does not break the basis. No approval is needed: it can be published freely. Such a work does not automatically enter the canon.
- **"Canon."** Status is granted after review (the procedure below). It is for those whose facts are to become part of the shared history.

A work that does not violate the physics but conflicts with the canon (a different date, a different name) can be read as a story from a neighboring universe of the layer: universes are similar but not identical (Section 1). This is not canon, but it is not rejected either. A work that violates the physics is not canonical and may not be called "written in this world."

**Two levels of canon:**
- **Hard canon** — the physics of the world, Parts I and II. Not debated, not voted on; changed only by a new version of the document (see "Versioning of the basis").
- **Soft canon** — facts and events: the chronicle below and everything that happens in canonical works (who betrayed whom, which colony fell, who discovered the equation).

**The approval criterion** is one and consists of two conditions: (1) the principles of Parts I and II are not violated; (2) there are no contradictions with already accepted canonical facts. Literary quality is not judged.

**The arbiter.**
- **Stage 1.** While the universe is young, the author of the concept applies the criterion alone.
- **Stage 2.** Once enough canonical works have accumulated, a collegium of authors who have earned trust is formed. Decisions on soft canon are made by the collegium under the same criterion (this is not a readers' vote). The author of the concept retains a veto over any decision that violates the physics of the world; the veto does not extend to the choice of soft canon.

**What is submitted for approval.** Not a manuscript, but a list of the facts the work establishes: dates, names, events, new consequences. A refusal always states which principle is violated or which fact it contradicts.

**Conflicts of facts.** If two works claim the same fact, the date of the pitch submission decides, not the date of publication. While a pitch is under review, the fact is reserved for its claimant.

**What counts as publication.** Any publicly available publication with a verifiable date: a post, a book, self-publishing, a game.

**Procedure:**
1. The author submits a pitch for canonicity with a list of the facts being established.
2. The arbiter (or the collegium) checks conformity to the physics.
3. The absence of contradictions with accepted facts, including the chronicle, is verified.
4. If there are contradictions, the author is asked to resolve them.
5. If it conforms, the work receives canonical status and its facts are entered into the chronicle.

**The chronicle — the core of soft canon.** These are the shared facts on which all works rely. For now the arbiter fills in the fields; an unfilled field is open for a pitch under the procedure above.

|---|---|
| Date of discovery of the multiversal layer | [set by the arbiter] |
| Who discovered it (the scientist's name) | [set by the arbiter] |
| Date of the first experimental transition (probe) | [set by the arbiter] |
| Date of the first ship transition | [set by the arbiter] |

### 40. The AI Assistant as Editor

The concept is written so that an AI assistant can help review works for canon conformity. Any AI loaded with this document will do; there is no dedicated tool for this. This is not censorship but advice: the AI flags contradictions, the author decides what to do with them.

**Three stages of review:**
- **Before writing.** The author describes the idea. The AI checks: does it violate the physics of the world? Does it contradict accepted facts? If so, it names the specific sections and suggests rethinking the idea.
- **During writing.** The author sends fragments. The AI checks the details: is the transition described correctly, has the trace been forgotten, has the vulnerability window been overlooked, is an anchor priced too cheaply. The AI does not write for the author, does not judge literary merit, and does not propose plots. It checks the mechanics.
- **After writing.** The AI performs a full review of the work. The result is a list of potential contradictions with references to sections of the document. The author resolves them or argues why the deviation is acceptable. The final decision rests with the arbiter.

**What the AI does not do:**
- Does not decide whether a work is canonical.
- Does not judge literary quality.
- Does not propose plots — for that there are the pitches (Section 38).
- Does not replace the arbiter.

### 41. Terms of Use

**This is a draft.** The terms are put up for discussion; the final text will be drawn up afterward. The figures below are guidelines, not decisions.

**Principle.** Any author, especially a beginning one, may write and publish stories in this world free of charge and without prior approval. If a work becomes commercially successful, a small royalty is paid to the world's author.

**Voluntary adoption.** The terms apply to those who themselves declare that a work is written in the world of "Quantum Paths" (the status "Written in this world," Section 39). ## Appendix. Scheme of the Mechanism and the Status of Claims

### A. Scheme of the Basic Mechanism

1. **The path exists before technology.** The multiversal layer contains physical paths between states that may be far apart in ordinary space.
2. **Spin links a state to the path.** The passage of a spin through the layer can have a manifestation in an individual universe.
3. **Natural entanglement is interuniversal.** This is a founding rule of this fictional model; ordinary within-universe entanglement almost never arises naturally.
4. **Artificial entanglement opens access.** A technology-made pair within a single universe sets the addressing condition for selecting a path. The corridor is access to an existing path, not a newly created object of space.
5. **A fluctuation is a weak trace.** If this universe has no corresponding stable entangled particle, the passage of a spin may leave a short-lived local manifestation.
6. **Second-order hypotheses.** The cosmological constant and the layer's gravitational effect are not part of the basis (Section 35).

### B. What Was Reconciled in Editing

- Natural interuniversal entanglement and the artificial entanglement of an anchor within a single universe are distinguished.
- Path and corridor are distinguished: the path is part of the layer's structure; the corridor is a regime of managed access to it.
- The transit anchor (external, in open space) and the link anchor (inside the communications module) are distinguished.
- The two modes of communication are distinguished: the atom-bit channel (equipment and a link anchor) and the packet probe (when there is no equipment).
- All states of an object are preserved relative to the anchor's frame of reference; there is no common frame of reference between different stars, and the ship's motion in the new system is determined by its gravity (Section 14).
- The unified temporal field is designated as a stand-alone postulate providing internal causality; it is not passed off as a consequence of relativity theory.

### C. The Status of Scientific Claims

All statements about the multiversal layer, spin as the carrier of the path, the special distribution of entanglement, fluctuations as traces of the spin's passage, and the interuniversal distribution of energy belong to the speculative make-up of the world. They do not describe established mechanisms of modern physics. In particular, real quantum entanglement can arise between systems within a single universe; the link between the observed cosmological constant and vacuum energy, and the nature of dark matter, remain unsolved fundamental problems. The text therefore does not confuse the internal truth of the world's rules with confirmation of those rules in reality.
